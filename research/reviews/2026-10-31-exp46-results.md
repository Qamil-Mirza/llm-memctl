## 4. Limitation of the probe arm (post hoc, labelled; the verdict above is not revised)

The dev probe fits base-case identity, not the supervisor's name, and the held read shows exactly that: a fall from
0.97 to 1/12 with held c1 also at 1/12. The cause is the case list, not the model. Dev has 6 RC base cases and 12
labels, each label occurring in one base only (B in v0/v2/v3, B′ in v1), so within dev the label is a function of (base,
variant) and no probe fitted on dev can separate "the supervisor's name" from "this base case". Rev 3 recorded the
confound and rev 5 removed the selectivity gate it broke; what rev 5 could not fix is that the held read, while a genuine
across-base test, tests a probe that had no across-base training signal. Two facts say the probe arm is uninformative
rather than negative: (i) at L31–35 the probe is at 1/12 although the dev-only §45 audit put the supervisor's name at
median rank 6 of 151k at the last prompt token at L33 (briefing 2026-10-30 §1b), so a readable signal exists there that
this probe cannot pick up; (ii) the same probe design at P_q is also at chance although the questioner's name is the
token under it. **"Not decodable at P_last before layer 30" is therefore not established as a property of the
representation; it is a property of this probe's training data.** The row-4 verdict is reported as the fixed rule
computed it; its first clause ("route late at P_last") rests on the probe arm and is marked **not established**; its
second clause ("instrument blind at P_B") rests on the intervention arm and stands. A probe arm that could decide
the first clause needs many base cases per label (names varied independently of everything else), i.e. a new case
list, not a re-fit on this one.
